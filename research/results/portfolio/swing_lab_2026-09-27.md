# Portfolio-cap swing lab — 26 symbols, 2016-2026 (2026-09-27)

> **Superseded by [swing_lab_v2_2026-09-27.md](swing_lab_v2_2026-09-27.md)**, which loosened the choices this v1 made on
> its own (one portfolio-wide bot, SL/TP-only exits) and found C3 (Gotobi JPY basket), passing its holdout.

**Verdict: no strategy found that clears the pre-registered bar.** Two configs land in the same direction
in-sample and out-of-sample (`trd` fast=10/slow=30/pull=5; `sqztrend` N=20/pct=30/ema=10-50) but both are
statistically weak (t < 1 in-sample) — not a confirmed edge, just the least-bad of ~95 configurations tried.
Everything else either never turns net-positive in-sample, or flips sign out-of-sample. This extends (does not
overturn) the project's standing finding — `research/README.md`: "no strategy tested so far has an edge that
survives multiple testing and out-of-sample years" — now checked against 2.7x more history, 11 more symbols,
and a materially different position-management style (see below) than every earlier lab run.

## Brief: what was asked and what was built

The ask was to research trading algorithms against the (now) 26-symbol dataset, with three constraints fixed
in advance, everything else my own call:

- Position size independent of account capital, but not frozen either — the algorithm may scale a trade up or
  down around a default lot.
- No position held longer than 5 days.
- A bot never holds more than 3 positions at once.

`research/scan/portfolio.py` (already sketched in an earlier, uncommitted pass on this branch, extended here)
implements exactly this: a single bot trading up to `CAP=3` symbols at once, every position force-exited at the
earliest of its stop, its target or 5 calendar days, sized by risking a constant `BASE_RISK_USD=$100` (the
"default lot") times a 0.5x-2x multiplier set by how strong this signal is *relative to this same rule's own
past signals on this symbol* — a causal expanding median, so the scaling only ever looks backward and never
depends on account equity. Admission across symbols is causal and cross-sectional: at every shared bar-close,
free slots go to the strongest fresh signals system-wide, ranked by that trade's own strength, never doubling
up on a symbol already held.

## Method: pre-registration, not just backtesting

Given how much of `STRATEGIES.md` is "looked promising, didn't survive": every family below was tuned on
**2016-01-01..2021-12-31 only** ("IS", 6 years). The best IS config per family was then checked once against
**2022-01-01..2026-09-24** ("OOS", ~4.75 years), never re-picked afterward. A trade admitted before a date never
depends on data after it, so bucketing one continuous cap=3/hold<=5d simulation's trades by `t_in` into IS/OOS
is equivalent to having run on truncated history for IS purposes. Reported alongside: a random-entry control
(20 seeds, same trade count per symbol, random side — the bar other strategies in this repo use, `>=95%` of
seeds beaten) and per-year sign consistency. In total **~95 parameter configurations across 9 distinct signal
families** were tried; with that many looks, a handful of t>=2 cells are expected by chance alone (the same
caveat `research/README.md` gives for the main lab's ~1200 cells) — which is exactly why the IS/OOS split, not
the pooled number, is the bar that matters here.

| mrv (RSI(2) extreme fade) | -13,465 | -0.044 | -3.2 | -7,600 | -5,866 | fail |

"Beats random" here only means less bad than a random entry with the same trade count — `brk` and `sqz` are
still net losers in dollars. None of the 5 is positive in a majority of years, and every one of them lost money
badly IS and only turned around OOS — exactly the pattern that must not be trusted without a prior IS signal
(full output: `research/results/portfolio/full_run_2026-09-27.md`).

## Four more ideas tried, and the parameter sweeps (~95 configs, IS 2016-21 -> OOS 2022-26)

- **`sig_trendbrk`** — the Donchian breakout above, taken only with an EMA(fast) vs EMA(slow) trend filter (to
  cut whipsaws). **No parameter combination is IS-positive** (best: N=100, ema 20/100, IS $-5,370); several
  configs *not* selected by IS look very good OOS (N=20, ema 20/100: OOS +$15,856, t+1.9) — a textbook
  data-snooping trap, reported here specifically as a warning against picking on OOS.
- **`sig_sqz_trend`** — the Bollinger squeeze breakout with the same trend filter. Best IS: N=20, pct=30,
  ema=10/50, IS +$5,203 (t+0.6, weak) -> OOS +$5,377 (t+0.4, weak). Same sign both sides, but neither half is
  individually significant — the closest thing to a "maybe" this sweep produced besides `trd`.
- **`sig_range_fade`** — fade a pullback from EMA(N) only when ADX says the market is *not* trending (a
  regime-filtered mean reversion, distinct from the RSI(2) fade). **Every one of 27 configs is negative both
  IS and OOS** (best IS: N=10, zmax=1.5, adx_max=15, still -$2,419) — the cleanest rejection in this batch:
  consistent sign in both eras across the whole grid means this is a real, not a noisy, negative expectancy
  once (real, from-tick) spread is paid.
- **`sig_tom`** (turn-of-month, indices only: DE40 UK100 US30 USTEC US500 JP225 AUS200 HK50) — long 1 trading
  day before month-end. Best IS (before=1): IS +$1,625 (t+1.8, the closest any config got to the t>=2 bar) ->
  OOS **-$208** (t-0.3): sign flips, fails confirmation. The classic equity turn-of-month drift (already
  flagged `+$5,651, rejected` in `STRATEGIES.md`'s TOM entry on the smaller universe/window) does not survive
  a proper pre-registered look here either.

Full grids: `research/results/portfolio/sweep_{mom,brk,trd,sqz,mrv,trendbrk,sqztrend,rangefade,tom}.csv`.

## Why this is probably genuinely hard, not just an unlucky search

1. **The 5-day cap is a headwind for exactly the family (trend-following) most likely to work on a diversified
   multi-asset basket over 10 years.** Documented CTA/trend premia (Moskowitz-Ooi-Pedersen and the industry's
   own 2022 "trend renaissance" during the rate-hiking cycle) earn their edge from convexity in the *tail* of a
   sustained multi-week/month move; forcing every position flat after 5 days regardless of how it is doing
   caps exactly that tail. `mom`/`trd`/`trendbrk` all show the same shape here: badly negative 2016-2021 (a
   low-rate, range-bound, mean-reversion-friendly regime), much better 2022-2026 (a genuine multi-year rates/
   USD/energy trend) — consistent with a regime effect the hold-cap structurally can't fully capture, not
   necessarily with these specific rules having an edge.
2. **Costs are real and dominant.** `research/README.md`'s memory note already found the pre-cost (mid-price)
   edge on this broker's ticks is close to zero and per-trade cost is 0.02-0.4R; every fill in this lab uses
   the real bid/ask from the tick cache, so a marginal signal (most of what showed up here, IS t rarely above
   1) is exactly the size costs can erase.
3. **Multiple testing cuts the other way too**: with ~95 looks, *not* finding an IS-significant, OOS-confirmed
   config is itself informative at reasonable power — it is not merely "try more params next time" territory
   without a genuinely different information source (e.g., a variable this repo doesn't have: real order flow,
   macro surprise data, or cross-asset carry from actual funding rates).

## Recommendation

Do not deploy any of these. If the next step is to keep pushing this specific angle, the two directions most
likely to move the needle, in order:

1. **Test `trd` (fast=10/slow=30/pull=5) and `sqztrend` (N=20/pct=30/ema=10-50) combined in one cap=3 bot**
   (both weakly positive, same sign IS+OOS, and built from different bar types/timeframes so plausibly
   under-correlated) — diversification could, in principle, lift pooled t past 2 without new information; this
   was not run here to avoid adding a 96th look after already seeing both individually, and should itself be
   pre-registered (one config, checked once) rather than swept.
2. **Loosen the 5-day cap for a trend-only bot and compare** — since (1) above suggests hold-cap-driven tail
   truncation may be the structural issue for `trd`/`mom`/`trendbrk` specifically, a controlled comparison
   (same rules, hold cap 5d vs 10d vs 20d, same IS/OOS discipline) would tell us whether that theory holds
   before spending more parameter-sweep budget on 5-day-capped trend variants.

Neither is a claim that either will pass; both are the most defensible next experiments given what this run
found, not a promise of an edge.
